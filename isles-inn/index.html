Why come to Skye

The Isle of Mist, from the middle of it all

Skye is the largest island of the Inner Hebrides, and Portree sits right at its centre. From the inn you can reach mountains, sea cliffs and quiet bays in under an hour, then come back to a coal fire and live music.

Landscapes like nowhere else

The Old Man of Storr and the Quiraing stand on the Trotternish ridge just north of Portree. To the south rise the jagged peaks of the Black Cuillin, a ridge climbers come to from all over the world.

Wildlife on the doorstep

Seals and otters live along the shore, and boat trips from Portree harbour go looking for sea eagles. Bring binoculars and patience.

Eilean a' Cheò

Gaelic, whisky and music

Gaelic is still part of island life, Talisker is distilled on the west coast, and through the season the bar at the inn hosts Scottish musicians from Skye and beyond.

Eat & drink

The bar and restaurant

Highland food, a good malt and a coal fire

We cook with the best produce Skye has to offer wherever we can. Pull up a chair by the open fire with a malt whisky or a local ale.

The bar shows live sport, from football and rugby to tennis, and through the season we have live Scottish music from local bands and from further afield.

Open daily

Bar and restaurant are open every day except Christmas Day. Check our social media for the latest times.

Table bookings are for guests staying at the inn. Everyone else is welcome to walk in.

From the door

Portree is the middle of Skye

The whole island is within reach for a day out, and the ferries to the Outer Hebrides are not far beyond.

Sligachan & Glenbrittle

The Cuillin

Skye's mountains, with walks for every level from Sligachan and Glenbrittle.

North-west Skye

Dunvegan Castle

Seat of the Clan MacLeod, with the Coral Beach a short drive further on.

Short ferry from Sconser

Isle of Raasay

A quiet island across the sound, with Dùn Caan's flat top rising above it.

Five minutes' walk

Portree harbour

The painted houses, the pier and the shops are all close by.

Also within a day: the Quiraing, Kilt Rock, Talisker Distillery, Armadale Castle and the Sleat peninsula, and on the mainland Eilean Donan Castle and Plockton.

Getting here

By road, rail or air

From Fort William

A82 north to Invergarry, then the A87 towards Kyle of Lochalsh. Cross the Skye Bridge (no toll) and follow the signs to Portree.

From Inverness

A82 towards Fort William, then right at Invermoriston onto the A887, which joins the A87 towards Kyle of Lochalsh.

By train

Main line to Inverness, then the Kyle line to Kyle of Lochalsh.

By air

Inverness Airport.

Parking

The inn has no car park of its own. There are pay and display and free car parks close by.
