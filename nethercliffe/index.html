Why come to Wick

The far north of Scotland, with a story in every street

Wick sits on the north-east tip of the mainland, where the North Coast 500 turns along the top of Scotland. It is a town built on the sea, and the Nethercliffe has been part of it for two hundred years.

c.1826

Built in the herring boom

The house went up while the harbour was being extended and Wick was growing into the busiest herring port in the world. Original cornicing survives, and the flagstone cellars, once the servants' quarters, still hold a cast-iron cooking range.

A dram with a connection

The ground was bought in 1823 by John Kirk, later Provost of Wick, who became a partner in founding the Pulteney Distillery in 1827. Drink an Old Pulteney in the garden of the house its founder built.

Cliffs, stacks and light nights

The Stacks of Duncansby, the Castle of Mey and the wild Caithness coast are a short drive away. In summer the days stretch and darkness is reluctant to fall.

Stay

Six generously sized rooms

Every room is en-suite, with a flat-screen digital TV, tea and coffee, a direct-dial telephone and free Wi-Fi. Bathrooms are stocked with toiletries and hairdryers.

Each room has plenty of local information, and the resident owners are always happy to help you plan your days.

Eat & drink

Home cooking and a warm lounge bar

Meals at the Nethercliffe are cooked in-house. The chef is fully trained in dietetics, so every dietary requirement can be catered for.

On summer nights the south-facing garden is a riot of colour and the place to sit with a glass of Old Pulteney, the local whisky.

Function hall

Room for around 50 for private parties, or about 60 theatre-style for small conferences and training. Menus and hot or cold buffets can be tailored to your budget.

From the door

Caithness, close up

Some of the most beautiful and unspoilt scenery in Scotland, and the owners have maps and walking trails to help you find it.

Short drive north

The Caithness coast

Sea arches, cliffs and the Stacks of Duncansby at the very corner of mainland Britain. Sea tours run along the coast too.

Around the county

Open country, forests and quiet beaches, with the past never far away.

From the door

Walks in and around Wick

In town

Pulteney Distillery

The most northerly distillery on the British mainland. Take the tour, taste, and bottle your own.

Also nearby: the award-winning Wick Heritage Centre, five minutes' walk away, and the Castle of Mey, once the Queen Mother's holiday home, open to visitors in summer.

Getting here

Easier than you think

By train

Wick station is a five-minute walk from the hotel.

By air

Wick John O'Groats Airport is one mile away.

By road

Wick is on the North Coast 500. There is a free public car park directly opposite the hotel.

By motorbike

Bikes can be parked in a secluded area of the hotel grounds.
